ABOUT THE COLLECTION

Early work. Enduring questions.

Poetry can be a record of what a person notices, questions, mourns, and still dares to hope for.

This collection brings together eight early poems by Liberian writer Theotis L. Sneh, first written and publicly shared from 2020 to 2022. The poems turn toward public life and private experience: political predation, the responsibilities of young people, gender equality, growth, bereavement, self-mastery, democratic hope, and the difficult choices that shape tomorrow.

This digital edition presents the poems in a calm, reader-focused format. The original dates and cultural expressions are retained, allowing readers to encounter the work in its historical sequence.

POEM 01
January 14, 2020

The Sharks

Politicians in Africa

The sharks—
With teeth sharp as razors,
Fins flapping in the dark,
Waiting for their prey
To devour like a quick meal.

Those who fall for the friendly smile
End up with blood
Running down their chins.
Fire ahead is all they can see.

This predator cannot stop
Hunting its prey.
The prey has almost ceased to feel,
For its wounds are not healing,
But expanding.

When will the shark
Cease its savagery?

I know the answer:

When its prey finds a way
To escape its path.

POEM 02
January 25, 2020

These Things

Breaking free from bondage,
Casting off the chains of slavery,
Working hard to achieve justice,
Yet transparency is bought,
While injustice is free.

The youth, the youth—

No longer expecting correction,
Like falcons no longer listening
To the falconer.

Role models have become
Key agents of corruption.
Revolution no longer sets its objectives
Without personal gain.

Everything is no longer
What it seems,
And advice has become
Mere words.

The youth, the youth—

With corrupt minds and souls,
Wondering what will become
Of this dark,
Rich,
And glorious continent.

POEM 03
May 3, 2020

Gallots and Feme

The gallot is supreme—
But who is the feme?

This marginalization
Makes the world unbalanced.

A bull and a cow, yoked together,
Can still work the field equally.
So why elevate one
Above the other?

If the gallot is served
At an ambrosial seat,
Why not the feme?

Why treat the feme
As if the world were made
Only for gallots
To control the hemisphere?

The feme’s time has come.
It is her time
To show what she is made of.

Because there is nothing
The gallot can do
That the feme cannot.

POEM 04
June 9, 2020

Bloom

Planted on solid ground,
Blooming toward the sun,
The conditions are perfect;
The best is yet to come.

With high hopes,
The sower waits
To see the best of his seeds.

He watches as they struggle
Through strong winds
And harsh radiation,
Yet they stand the test of time.

He feeds them
With the best fertilizer.
He molds them
Into the best they can be.

The harvest season has come.

His hard work
Has brought him baskets of plenty,
Enough for his household
To feed.

POEM 05
January 11, 2021

The Puzzle

Life is a puzzle
Waiting to be solved.

This singular expression
Is so deep,
Yet it can never be completed.

Lost in my dreams,
You have fallen asleep.

Only the departed
Can explain
Your state of being.

You have shuffled off
This mortal coil,
And I failed to see it
Until it came.

And now,
The puzzle is incomplete.

Dejected, my heart mourns;
To mourn is to know
The weight of loss.

I look to the sky,
And your smile I see.

Now, in Paradise,
We shall meet.

POEM 06
August 18, 2021

Reputed

Let them see my battles fought,
My pursuit of success.

When success meets the man who strives,
Recession falls upon him who sleeps.

But I write of him who strives
And struggles on through weary years,
Against himself,
The prize still shining ahead.

Resilient and laudable is he;
He fights foes unseen.
Though thorns lie beneath his feet,
His passion keeps his faith.

He stands erect among his peers,
Never apprehensive of the future—
The bravest of men
I have seen.

Through manhood,
He shall conquer.

Poor or rich he may be,
But truth be told:

He who conquers himself
And becomes free
Is a legend.

POEM 07
January 30, 2022

We Vote, We Hope

Is it hard to vote
After all I’ve got?

Is it hard to cope
After the job I lost?

Why must I cry
When the time is mine?
Why must I die
While my child lives in crime?

Is this the freedom
For which I strive and starve?
Will this widow find a job
At a fair price?

Fear lives among the thugs,
Beer clouds the mind,
And teenagers are caught
In the love of money
For smiles.

Grown in joy
From parents sick in the mines,
Still no job—
Yet hope remains
That the vote will change lives.

A crown rises
From the past.

Freedom!
Nelson!
Black people, be proud.

Education is the key
That opens the mind.
Killing blindness
Can change our times.

Success has no heart to find,
While poverty becomes a ladder
For those with determined minds.

The power.
The blood.
The darker side.

The calmer the crowd,
The thinner the child.

Jobs are hard to find,
And the bread keeps changing sides.

Voting daily, always proud;
Finding freedom, always hard.

“Mayibuye iAfrika!”

With these words,
We break the ties.

Bringing life
When we fight.

Let us work together
And love one another,
Until hunger
Finds no home among us.

God bless Africa.

POEM 08
February 20, 2022

The Wayfarer

Cold as the breath
Of the Arctic wind,
My dry skin
Like the breeze of the Sahara—

Creeping memories
Of yesterday’s failures.

Today’s rejections
Are like dark rooms,
Filled with a mist
Of blind adventure,
As I search
For hidden light switches.

I see two paths ahead.

My heart leaps
In quantum fear,
And the routes to tomorrow
Grow narrower.

The other path
Glitters with gold,
But it is filled
With crooks.

Today’s decisions
Affect tomorrow.

So, person weh go make better choice,
Na him go find joy.

The ending.

AUTHOR’S NOTE

Words from an early chapter.

These poems trace an early period of reflection on African society, political corruption, youth and leadership, gender equality, perseverance, grief, democracy, poverty, personal struggle, and hope. The revised edition is intended to make the work more accessible while preserving its central themes, cultural expressions, and early poetic voice.

— Theotis L. Sneh
